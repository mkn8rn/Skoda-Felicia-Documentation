Caddy Pickup July 1996 running-gear specifications

The manufacturer describes its source-specific suspension, wheel and brake assemblies without complete physical part numbers.

Front suspension and wheel class

The front suspension uses MacPherson struts, transverse lower arms and large rubber arm-to-carrier bushes. The stabilizer diameter is 22 mm. The technical fields give 2,450 mm wheelbase, 1,420 mm front track and an 11.2 m turning circle. The stated steel rim class is 5.5 J ×13 with 36 mm offset, paired with 165/80 R13 tyres.[1]

Steering assistance belongs to the manufacturer’s base vehicle concept; exact rack/pump identity and hydraulic interfaces are Unconfirmed.[3]

Rim stock number, H-profile, tyre load/speed ratings, pressure, chain conditions and the selected axle/spring products remain Unconfirmed. These values do not replace the separate national tyre-approval selectors.

Rear suspension

The rear is a twist-beam assembly with separate coil springs and dampers, plus rubber auxiliary springs. The springs and dampers are separated to preserve load-platform width. The brackets permit longitudinal and transverse axle positioning; the stated rear track is 1,384 mm. This description does not supply alignment limits or an adjustment procedure.[1]

Standard brake architecture

Braking has two diagonally divided hydraulic circuits and a 9-inch servo. Front brakes use floating-caliper discs; rear drums have automatic adjustment. A mechanical load-dependent pressure regulator serves the standard architecture. Disc/drum/caliper/cylinder dimensions and full hydraulic product identities are Unconfirmed.[1]

Optional Mark 20 GI architecture

The optional ABS is named Mark 20 GI. Its electronic brake-force distribution replaces the mechanical load regulator, which is omitted with this option. The publication separately identifies the ABS controller, pump/valve hydraulic unit and extra engine-bay fuses. It provides no exact controller, modulator, sensor/ring, calibration or loom identity. The name does not establish a shared physical product with another Mark 20 application.[1][2]

Felicia AutoWeek test equipment and vehicle ratings

AutoWeek’s four Dutch road tests identify separate body, grade and engine descriptions. Observed test equipment, reported grade equipment and current archive specification boxes have different evidential scope; none supplies an original factory-parts selection.

Transmission descriptions

Each identification box records front-wheel drive and a five-speed manual transmission. Gear ratios, gearbox product, clutch, final drive and exact mounting/shaft selection remain Unconfirmed.[1][2][3][4]

Combi LXD observations

The diesel test describes assisted steering, front discs and rear drums, metallic-black paint, front and outer-rear head restraints, an unequally divided folding/removable rear bench and a luggage cover. Steering-assistance architecture, component products and colour code remain Unconfirmed.[1]

The report says the tested/basic diesel specification lacked a tachometer, central locking, electric windows and a cigarette lighter. These reported absences do not establish the equipment of every LXD/LX diesel or zero original quantities across a factory production range.[1]

Combi GLXi equipment and extras

The report describes roof rails, velour upholstery, four head restraints, central locking of the four side doors, a tachometer, tinted glazing and continuously variable wiper intervals as GLXi equipment. It describes a removable two-piece luggage cover and an unequally split folding rear bench whose portions can be removed. The head-restraint positions and individual glazing, wiper-control and locking products remain Unconfirmed.[3]

The tested car additionally had a sliding roof and importer-fitted alloy wheels with wider tyres written as 185-55. The tyre’s rim diameter, full load/speed designation, wheel dimensions and product identities are not supplied. The current archive box instead lists 165/70R13; that entry does not identify the importer-installed test set. The sliding roof’s supplier, drive mechanism and factory-versus-later installation remain Unconfirmed. Steering effort is discussed, without an exact rack or installed assistance specification.[3]

Later Combi GLX observations

The GLX report describes the revised front appearance, a hard smooth plastic parking-brake grip and a liftgate-release lever beside the driver’s seat. Rear-seat portions fold and can be removed; the stated luggage volume below the cover is 447 litres. Physical trim, release mechanism, body revision and production limits remain Unconfirmed.[4]

The hatchback narrative reports 11.6 km/litre during the test and a separate 16 km/litre claimed European-cycle figure. The diesel narrative reports 18.5 km/litre on a gentle journey, about 12 under heavy use and 15 overall, while its current box lists 6.4 litres/100 km. The earlier GLXi Combi narrative reports 11.6 km/litre during testing; both petrol Combi boxes list 6.9 litres/100 km. These differing test/cycle descriptions are not directly equivalent or guaranteed consumption.[1][2][3][4]

Physical selection

The title-year labels differ from the archive’s displayed dates. Neither establishes actual manufacture, original test date, national-grade equivalence, steering side, factory options or chassis/build selection. Test observations do not supply complete engine/gearbox identities, OE numbers/suffixes, original quantities or service supersessions. An importer-installed accessory is not proof of original factory fitment.

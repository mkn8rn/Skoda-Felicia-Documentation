Felicia body shells and cabin arrangements

The hatchback, passenger Combi, commercial Pickup, Vanplus and Fun have different body and cabin arrangements. Structural descriptions and production-period revisions do not identify one interchangeable shell or a complete panel application.

Hatchback shell adaptations

Škoda’s 2014 Felicia retrospective places a first package of significant changes in 1996, including reinforcement of the body structure and multipoint injection for the 1.3-litre engine. It does not identify affected panels or give a body-specific chassis-number limit for the hatchback; code Š 791 alone therefore does not identify a reinforced-body parts range.[1]

A 2001 technical account describes a separate shell distinction for hatchbacks. Unadapted shells intended for cars without airbag provision were produced through December 1994. Production of shells adapted for airbags, air conditioning and Volkswagen engines began in January 1995 alongside them; unadapted production ended in July 1995, after which the account describes all shells as adapted. It also associates front and floor reinforcement with airbag-equipped cars and Volkswagen 1.6 MPI or 1.9 D engines. Škoda’s retrospective separately dates a body-structure change package to 1996. Neither account identifies changed panels or explains whether the 1996 package was an additional revision to the earlier shell adaptation. These are production-period descriptions, not VIN cutoffs or complete parts applications.[2][1]

Combi shell adaptations

Škoda’s 2014 Felicia retrospective places body-structure reinforcement within an initial 1996 change package for the model family. It does not list affected Combi panels or provide a Combi-specific chassis-number boundary; type Š 795 alone cannot identify a particular structural-parts revision.[1]

A 2001 technical account describes the Combi as using its own extended shell and states that Combi shells were produced with structural adaptations from their introduction in May 1995. The same account associates front and floor-area reinforcement with airbag provision and Volkswagen 1.6 MPI or 1.9 D engines. It does not identify Combi-specific panels or show whether the manufacturer’s 1996 change package was additional to these earlier shell adaptations. The production chronology does not supply a VIN or parts-number boundary.[2][1]

Pickup shell adaptations

A 2001 technical account distinguishes the two-seat Pickup shell from the short passenger shell and the longer Combi/Vanplus shell group. It states that Pickup shells used the adapted structure from the model’s August 1995 introduction; it also describes front and floor-area reinforcement for airbag equipment and Volkswagen 1.6 MPI or 1.9 D engines. Škoda’s retrospective separately places body-structure reinforcement in a 1996 change package. The sources do not identify Pickup-specific panels or reconcile whether the 1996 package describes an additional revision, so neither type 797 nor a sales date establishes a complete Pickup parts application.[2][1]

Vanplus shell scope

A 2001 technical account groups the Combi and Vanplus as using extended body shells, distinct from the short hatchback and two-seat Pickup. It gives an adapted-shell start date for the Combi and Pickup, but does not assign that same production date or structural parts limit to Vanplus. The Vanplus body therefore cannot be classified by applying the Combi chronology alone.[2]

Škoda dates the Felicia family facelift to February 1998. The retrospective does not identify the Vanplus body’s styling or structural changes, nor give a Vanplus chassis-number limit for the update. Its 795 VANPLUS designation does not by itself identify pre- and post-facelift parts fitment.[3]

Passenger-body facelift dimensions

Škoda dates the passenger-body facelift to February 1998. Its 2024 account reports a 28 mm length increase for the hatchback and a 32 mm increase for the Combi. The same account gives the estate’s pre-facelift length as 4,205 mm. Continued use of types 791 and 795 does not identify revised panels or establish interchangeability, and the passenger-body account does not establish a Pickup or Vanplus revision.[4][3]

Fun cabin and load bed

The Fun retained an open pickup load bed and a yellow-accented exterior, but its cabin was designed for occasional rear passengers. In its normal position, the movable rear wall separates the two-seat cab from the load area. Sliding it rearward exposes a compact rear bench, producing a four-seat 2+2 arrangement; with the bench in use, the usable bed length falls from 1,370 mm to 850 mm. Škoda’s 2020 Fun brief gives exterior dimensions of 4,245 × 1,680 × 1,465 mm and a 2,450 mm wheelbase. These are that historical specification’s figures, rather than a dimensional selection for every market or approval execution.[5][6]

March 1995 GLXi

The technical panel specifies a dry single-plate clutch, five-speed manual transmission and front-wheel drive. The dimensionless ratios are source-scoped to this test specification: first 3.31:1, second 1.91:1, third 1.27:1, fourth 0.93:1, fifth 0.72:1, reverse 2.92:1 and final drive 4.16:1. No physical gearbox family, assembly suffix or clutch spline/diameter is identified.[1]

The panel gives front suspension struts with triangular lower arms, a semi-rigid rear axle, rear coil springs and telescopic dampers, front disc/rear drum brakes and a mechanical parking brake acting on the rear wheels. The disc description does not supply an independently identified ventilation/product selection. Rack steering is stated, but the prose says the steering has no assistance while the panel states servo assistance; the actual assisted/unassisted rack selection is Unconfirmed.[1]

The stated rim class is 4.5 J × 13 and road tyre size is 165/70 R13, with Matador named without a product model, load/speed symbols or production code. The battery is described as 12 V / 44 Ah and the charging alternator as 55 A. Measurement conditions, case/terminal/pulley/offset dimensions, exact products and spare-wheel/tyre applications are Unconfirmed.[1]

June 1995 Combi launch

The grouped table describes front suspension struts with triangular arms and a stabiliser, a semi-rigid rear axle with coil springs, telescopic dampers and a stabiliser, front disc/rear drum brakes, rack steering, 4.5 J × 13 rims and 165/70 R13 tyres. Five gears are listed; the driven GLX 1.6 is separately described with a strengthened synchronised gearbox. No complete transmission, suspension, brake, rim or tyre product is identified.[2]

The driven GLX 1.6 is described without a steering servo. The table’s ABS notation and the separately described surcharge airbag classes do not establish installed ABS on that car or on the LX/SLX offers. The launch account names the Teves Mark 20 family for the described optional ABS system. The Slovenian prices were not established at publication; launch observations are not national grade-wide factory application limits.[2]

May 1995 GLXi/GLX test

The Gorenjski glas test names front fog lamps, a tachometer, an extended centre console, a cigar lighter, an interior tailgate-release lever and a divided rear bench. The roof radio aerial is described toward the rear. These observations do not identify lamp, instrument, console, latch, bench or aerial stock numbers or establish their fitment across every Slovenian grade.[3]

The reported five-door/five-seat test has dimensions 3,850 ×1,630 ×1,410 mm, wheelbase 2,450 mm and luggage capacity 272/967 litres. The manufacturer/test maximum speeds are separately reported as 150/154 km/h; the reported ECE consumption figures are 5.4, 7.4 and 7.9 litres/100 km, with 7.8 litres/100 km in the test. Those figures are source observations rather than part dimensions or identification of every gearbox or suspension variant.[3]

The May test selection keeps the headline GLXi and prose GLX label discrepancy explicit. Its grade label does not merge it with the separately dated March test.

November 1995 Combi GLXi test

The reported car uses a five-speed manual transmission and front-wheel drive. Its tachometer, front-bumper fog lamps, interior tailgate-release control, rear-roof radio aerial and alloy wheels are described without complete component products or loose quantities. The radio preparation is not an identified receiver. The rear bench folds for the reported larger cargo volume; its division and stock identity are unspecified. The two-part luggage cover is a described cover arrangement, not two separately numbered pieces.[4]

August 1999 Slovenian Fun test

The Slovenian Fun test reports five-speed manual transmission, front-wheel drive and anti-lock braking. Its movable rear partition provides two additional folding seating positions. The load-floor strips are described as wood, without a species or stock identity. A plastic arch above the rear opening is not a specified competition cage or verified underlying structural-frame material.[5]

Yellow leather trim is reported on the seats, door trim, wheel rim and gear-change lever; coloured plastic extensions surround the sills and wheel arches. The rear spoiler and alloy wheels lack complete product or geometry identifiers. The seating count does not identify loose covers, cushions, hinges or belt components.[5]

December 1996 covered Pickup test

The covered Pickup test reports five-speed manual transmission, front-wheel drive, steering assistance and a plastic cargo cover attributed to Veplas in Velenje. The cover and its shell describe one body-cover arrangement, not an additive count of independent complete covers. Composition, mounting, seals, factory option and product identities remain unconfirmed.[6]

February 1997 GLX diesel hatchback test

The report identifies five-speed manual transmission, front-wheel drive and standard diesel steering assistance. Its central locking excludes the tailgate from operation; the interior release, tachometer and front fog lamps have no complete product identifiers. Tinted glazing and radio preparation are offered choices, not proven fitted equipment.[7]

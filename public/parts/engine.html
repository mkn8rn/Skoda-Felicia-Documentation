Engines

The production Felicia range used three engine families: 1.3-litre petrol, 1.6-litre petrol, and 1.9-litre diesel. Škoda records 40 kW and 50 kW versions of the 1.3-litre engine.[1] Its 2015 Polish history dates the 55 kW 1.6 MPI to 1995, with its debut in the Combi, and the 47 kW 1.9 D to 1996.[2] A 2014 retrospective groups both larger engines among additions associated with the 1995 season and says the diesel followed the 1.6 later, without assigning it a separate year.[3] A 2019 manufacturer retrospective groups both larger engines among additions in 1995; these retrospective dates do not establish introduction dates in every body and national market.[4] MPI means multipoint fuel injection.

Engine layout and terminology

All three families have four cylinders in one row, operate on a four-stroke cycle, and use liquid cooling. The engine sits across the front of the vehicle, with the transmission on its left. Left and right refer to the vehicle as viewed from a seated occupant facing forward. The firing sequence is 1–3–4–2. Cylinder 1 is nearest the crankshaft pulley on the 1.3-litre engine and nearest the timing belt on AEE and AEF engines.[5][6][7]

Bore is the cylinder’s internal diameter; stroke is the piston’s travel between its end positions. Displacement is the combined swept volume of the cylinders. Compression ratio compares a cylinder’s volume with its piston at the bottom and top of that travel; it is a dimensionless ratio, not a compression-test pressure. OHV means overhead valves; SOHC means a single overhead camshaft.

1.3-litre petrol

Škoda’s retrospective dates the 1.3 MPI introduction to 1996.[3]

Displacement is 1,289 cm³, with a 75.50 mm bore and 72.00 mm stroke. The B-coded 135B and 136B engines use single-point injection; the M-coded 135M and 136M use multipoint injection. A 2015 technical table gives compression ratios of 8.8:1 for 135B, 9.7:1 for 136B, 9.5:1 for 135M and 10.0:1 for 136M. Its figures are identified as applicable to the Felicia vehicle specification of February 1997.[8] Single-point injection meters fuel at a shared throttle body; multipoint injection uses a separate injector at each intake port.[5]

Haynes manual 3505 gives 8.8:1 and 9.7:1 for the 135 and 136 engine groups without separate B- and M-code entries.[5] Those family-level figures do not identify the MPI ratios; the dated technical table lists the code-specific values separately.[8]

Some export markets also received a carburettor-fed 1.3-litre engine. Škoda’s retrospective describes export calibrations rated at 57 or 58 PS; it does not identify a single Felicia-wide output or code for this version.[2]

The OHV engine has an aluminium-alloy block, head, and rocker cover. Replaceable cast-iron cylinder liners form the bores. Seals at the liners’ lower ends separate coolant from the sump. Three main bearings support the crankshaft. A double-row chain connects the crankshaft to the camshaft in the block; the oil pump is driven through the distributor shaft. The 135 and 136 versions differ in their camshafts and piston crowns: 135 pistons have a recess, while 136 pistons have flat crowns.[5]

1.6-litre petrol

Škoda’s 1995 technical specification for the Š 791 1.6 MPI records the AEE engine with a five-speed manual gearbox and front-wheel drive.[2]

The AEE engine displaces 1,598 cm³. Its bore is 76.5 mm, stroke 86.9 mm, and compression ratio 9.8:1. It combines a cast-iron block with an aluminium-alloy head. Five main bearings support the crankshaft. A toothed belt drives the single camshaft in the head and the coolant pump. The camshaft operates the valves through hydraulic tappets, which compensate for valve clearance using engine oil. A separate chain from the crankshaft drives the sump-mounted oil pump.[6]

1.9-litre diesel

The AEF engine displaces 1,896 cm³, with a 79.5 mm bore, 95.5 mm stroke, and 22.5:1 compression ratio. Its block is cast iron and its head aluminium alloy. Fuel injection is indirect: fuel enters a swirl chamber in the head before combustion spreads into the cylinder. The head contains replaceable swirl chambers and a single camshaft acting through hydraulic tappets.[7]

The crankshaft has five main bearings. The timing belt drives the camshaft and an intermediate shaft. The intermediate shaft supplies the drive for the oil pump and brake-servo vacuum pump; the coolant pump uses the auxiliary belt. Oil jets below the cylinders cool the piston undersides, and an engine-coolant heat exchanger cools the oil.[7]

Fuel-system identification

The authorised February 1997 technical specification associates 135B and 136B with Bosch Mono-Motronic, and 135M and 136M with Siemens SIMOS 2P. Its 1.6 MPI 55 kW entry names Magneti Marelli 1AV; the 1.9 D 47 kW entry identifies a Lucas DPC distributor pump.[8]

These names identify technical families. They do not identify controller software, injector stock numbers or pump calibration, nor establish those physical parts for every market, production revision or vehicle.

Caddy Pickup July 1996 technical engines

Volkswagen’s July 1996 Caddy Pickup specification identifies AEE petrol and AEF diesel engines. AEE is a 1,598 cm³ inline four with 9.8:1 compression and Magneti Marelli 1AV management. It records 55 kW at 4,500 rpm and 135 Nm at 3,500 rpm, with more than 120 Nm between 2,000 and 4,300 rpm. The stated fuel is 95 RON unleaded; 91 RON is permitted as a substitute with reduced power.[53]

The AEF entry is a 1,896 cm³ naturally aspirated swirl-chamber diesel with 22.5:1 compression and a Lucas distributor injection pump. It records 47 kW at 4,300 rpm and 124 Nm at 3,000 rpm, with more than 120 Nm between 2,000 and 3,500 rpm; the minimum stated cetane number is 49. The Lucas name does not identify a DPC subtype, calibration or complete pump product.[53]

These entries identify the AEE technical selection and the AEF technical selection. They are distinct from the later UK brochure’s diesel ratings; differing rpm entries do not by themselves prove a production change. Exact engine and injection-assembly product numbers/suffixes and manufacturing application remain Unconfirmed.

It gives 13.7 mkg at 3500 rpm, while the adjoining driving prose gives 3750 rpm. That disagreement is not resolved into a factory calibration or product distinction. The account identifies Volkswagen engine origin without supplying a manufacturer engine code, a complete assembly or a proven SLX pairing.[34]

Budapest 1995 report: engine outputs

The show report names LX at 40 kW, and LXi/GLXi at 50 kW. It gives no displacement, power-speed standard, complete engine code, gearbox or grade-to-body mapping. Its written injection/catalyst description does not identify a full fuel-metering product or calibration.[35]

Portuguese 1997–1998 advertised engine-capacity classes

The named hatchback and Break 1.3 GLXi offers each state 1289 cm³; the two 1.9 GLXD offers each state 1896 cm³. The capacity boxes give no output, full engine code, fuel-metering product or calibration. The grade lettering and a later unallocated range promotion do not establish an MPI, AEE or AEF product mapping.[36]

Detzner Felicia rally construction

The team describes a specific Felicia competition conversion, rather than a standard road specification or one interchangeable physical assembly. Its component brands and modification classes do not supply full stock/OE identities.[1]

Engine and control

The reported transverse-front petrol four-cylinder uses increased compression, lightened masses, a reworked head, changed camshaft, adjustable camshaft wheel, programmable SPITRONICS multipoint-injection control and a sports air-filter selection. The throttle is recorded as 46 mm; output is reported as 75 kW at 7,000 rpm. Contactless programmable ignition uses Hall sensing and rotating distribution.[1]

Exact engine code, displacement, compression value, machining dimensions, lightened members, cam profile, ECU model/board/calibration and measured output certification are Unconfirmed.[1]

Transmission

The team describes a five-speed gearbox with shorter gearing, straight-cut teeth and a locking differential, paired with a sports clutch. Gear ratios, differential/clutch products, complete gearbox code and engine-interface geometry are Unconfirmed.[1]

Suspension

The team records reinforced front arms and HP Sporting suspension units at both axles, with independent front suspension and a twist-beam rear arrangement. Spring/damper products, geometry, loads, travel and attachment dimensions are Unconfirmed.[1]

Body and protection

The self-supporting body is partly lightened and has reinforced suspension attachments and aluminium underbody protection. The team’s photographs show a five-door hatchback; the photo badge differs from the written GLX designation. Neither proves original grade equipment, shell revision, replacement panel identities or manufacturing limits.[1][2]

Exhaust

The recorded exhaust uses a tubular/header manifold, a converter described as a series-production catalyst and a REMUS silencer. Full product identities and the catalyst’s original road-vehicle application are Unconfirmed.[1]

Brakes

The team records two hydraulic circuits, ventilated front discs, series LUCAS calipers, steel-braided flexible lines and Ferodo pads. Rear brakes are described as series equipment with a mechanically operated parking brake. Full caliper/disc/pad/hose identities, dimensions, rear architecture and original road application are Unconfirmed.[1]

Safety equipment

The described equipment comprises a Wiechers cage under DMSB self-build rules, two handheld extinguishers described as 2 kg units, RRS six-point harnesses, an electrical emergency cutoff and OMP sports seats. The team calls the seats and harnesses FIA-homologated; exact products, certificates and approval dates are Unconfirmed. Extinguishing medium, the basis of the nominal capacity, attachment locations, seat/occupant selections and cutoff wiring are Unconfirmed.[1]

Cockpit and recording accessories

The recorded accessories are a MOMO three-spoke wheel, OMP helmet box, Terraphone intercom, Terratrip distance counter and Drift HD 1080p camera programme. Exact product/revision identities, quantities, power supplies, sensing/calibration, mounting and interface details are Unconfirmed.[1]

Application limits

The reported first-registration date is not a conversion or build date. A/F rally classification does not identify a particular FIA Felicia form or exact approved installation. Competition equipment is separate from factory original fitment; no complete-engine, chassis or road-equipment interchange is established.[1]

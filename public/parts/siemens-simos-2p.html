Siemens SIMOS 2P injection

Siemens SIMOS 2P is the multipoint petrol-injection family specified for Felicia engines 135M and 136M in the authorised February 1997 technical data. The family name does not distinguish the physical controller and its calibration for the two output versions.[1]

Metering

FIA extension A-5528 13/02 ET identifies the Felicia MPI 1.3 with SIMOS 2P electronic injection. It records four effective fuel outlets in the intake manifold and a 45 ± 0.25 mm throttle passage. The extension’s validity begins on 1 March 1997; this is a homologation date, not a factory chassis-number cutoff.[2]

Fuel and vapour circuits

The extension’s schematic places the fuel pump inside the tank. A filter lies in the supply path to the fuel rail, which feeds the manifold injectors. A pressure regulator is associated with the rail. Fuel vapour has a separate path through an activated-carbon canister and a purge valve to the intake. The diagram establishes these component positions and connections; it does not specify their stock numbers, hose dimensions or regulated fuel pressure.[2]

Sensors and ignition

The controller receives crankshaft position, coolant temperature, manifold pressure, throttle position, exhaust oxygen and knock signals. The schematic identifies the controller, regulated throttle body, fuel injectors and an ignition rail containing a twin-spark coil. Spark plugs are shown downstream of that ignition assembly. Article 330 of the same extension specifies zero ignition distributors.[2]

Applications and physical selection

SIMOS 2P application evidence
135M and 136MFamily classification in the February 1997 technical specification[1]
A-5528 extension 13/02 ETFelicia MPI 1.3 homologation with the metering and circuit specifications above[2]

The extension does not map its configuration to either 135M or 136M, a national trim, a gearbox product or a production/VIN range. Controller hardware, software, injector and sensor stock numbers, connector revisions and engine-specific calibration remain Unconfirmed. The shared system name does not establish interchangeability of those physical parts.

Models and identification

The Škoda Felicia range comprised a five-door hatchback, a passenger estate and commercial pickup, with Vanplus and Fun derivatives. A factory body type, an engine code, a national sales grade and a homologation variant identify different aspects of a vehicle. None is interchangeable with the others: two Felicias with the same engine or broad type can have different cabin layouts, safety equipment and fitted parts.[1][2][3]

Body styles

The hatchback and Combi are passenger bodies. The ordinary Pickup has a two-seat cab and an open load platform. The factory passenger-body numbers Š 791 and Š 795 differ from national and EC approval identifiers.[2][3]

Škoda’s 2019 production account gives 915,843 hatchbacks, 351,905 Combi estates and 124,565 Pickups. It combines Fun and Vanplus as 9,176 vehicles; the 2020 Fun history divides that total into 4,016 Fun and 5,160 Vanplus vehicles. These figures sum to 1,401,489 Škoda-branded Felicias. Nearly 19,000 Volkswagen Caddy pickups, built between 1996 and 2000, are additional to that total.[4][5]

Factory derivatives and export designation

Vanplus changes the enclosed Combi body into a commercial vehicle. Fun changes the Pickup cabin into a recreational 2+2 arrangement. Combi and Vanplus share the base number 795; Pickup and Fun share 797. The derivative designation and the corresponding approval family distinguish them.[3][6][7]

Volkswagen Caddy was an export sales designation for Felicia-derived pickups in selected markets. It is not a fourth Felicia body style; the Felicia-derived vehicle must be distinguished from other Volkswagen vehicles carrying the Caddy name.[7]

Body and engine configurations

For the 1.3-litre road engine, codes 135B and 136B identify single-point injection at 40 and 50 kW; 135M and 136M identify multipoint injection at the same respective outputs.[8] AEE identifies the 55 kW 1.6 MPI petrol engine and AEF the 47 kW 1.9-litre indirect-injection diesel. Availability and grade names depend on the body, market and dated specification.[9][2]

The Dutch January 1995 LXi hatchback had the 40 kW single-point-injection engine. The 1999 Dutch LXi used the 50 kW multipoint-injection engine. A trim suffix such as “i” therefore does not define an engine output across countries and years.[10][11]

Production revisions and identification

Škoda dates the passenger-car facelift to February 1998. The hatchback became 28 mm longer and the Combi 32 mm longer. Continued use of body types 791 and 795 does not establish interchangeability of every panel or assembly before and after the change. A family-wide facelift account does not establish a Pickup or Vanplus body revision.[1]

Dated market offers distinguish sales grades and equipment choices. An approval revision identifies a regulatory configuration; its validity date is not a production/VIN limit. Original-part applicability can also depend on gearbox identity, steering side, safety equipment and factory option or chassis limits.[12]

Dated market and equipment configurations

Trim names and engine pairings varied by country and offer date. Each dated offer applies to its stated market. Factory restraint choices also distinguish cars with the same body, grade and engine; the Czech 1998 airbag offers and Dutch 1999 grade matrix use different equipment combinations.[14][11]

Fun and Vanplus configurations

The Felicia Fun offers and two Vanplus seating arrangements are distinct factory configurations. Škoda’s account gives series-level engine, output and drivetrain information for Fun, but does not publish per-engine production limits; the Vanplus history does not map seating to powertrain combinations.[6][7]

Homologation configurations

RDW’s EC-approval data distinguish four Felicia Combi variant/execution combinations under e11*93/81*0019*08. All four execution records have revision 1 and validity beginning on 30 April 1999. The engine columns establish the particular engine and output; the approval data do not assign those executions to the LX, LXi, GLX or GLXi sales grades.[22][23][24][25]

Motorsport models

The Felicia Kit Car was a Škoda Motorsport competition car, not a passenger-production trim. Motorsport engine types and outputs belong to the competition specification and are separate from road-car engine combinations.

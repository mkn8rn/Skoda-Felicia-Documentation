Škoda Felicia hatchback

The original Felicia was a five-door hatchback, factory type Š 791. Škoda records its public presentation in Prague on 26 October 1994. Its Polish importer dates the first series-built car to 5 September, while a later manufacturer anniversary source gives 17 October as the start of series production. The dated accounts thus distinguish the production milestone differently; both place the public launch in October. The hatchback was the passenger body from which the Combi and commercial derivatives expanded the range.[1][2]

Identification

Š 791 is the factory model code for the five-door hatchback. In later German type-approval records it appears as Felicia type 791, with approval family G952 and EC approval e11*93/81*0011. The Combi is separately classified as 795. Pickup and Fun share the broad type number 797, but their approval and body designations distinguish the two derivatives.[3]

The body is a self-supporting five-door passenger shell with a rear liftgate. Later Škoda rescue documentation calls the hatchback series 6U1; that later service identifier is different from the original factory model code Š 791.[4]

Factory powertrain range

The initial 1.3-litre petrol engine used Bosch single-point injection and was rated at 40 or 50 kW. Škoda records 57 and 58 PS carburettor calibrations for some export markets. The 1.3 MPI versions arrived in 1996, at the same two rated outputs. Felicia engine codes identify fuel-supply and compression versions: 135B and 136B identify single-point-injection versions, while 135M and 136M identify multipoint-injection versions. A code or output is not a body identifier.[2][5][9]

The AEE 1.6 MPI petrol engine is rated at 55 kW. Škoda’s Polish importer dates its Felicia introduction to 1995 and records its first appearance with the Combi; a 2019 manufacturer retrospective lists the engine among additions in 1995. The naturally aspirated AEF 1.9 D is rated at 47 kW; Škoda Polska dates it to 1996, whereas the 2019 retrospective groups it with 1995 additions. These family-wide dates do not establish that every market offered each powertrain in the hatchback from the same month.[1][2][6]

A Dutch Škoda importer specification dated 1999 lists seven hatchback combinations: LX with 1.3 MPI at 40 kW, 1.6 MPI at 55 kW or 1.9 D at 47 kW; LXi with 1.3 MPI at 50 kW; GLXi with 1.3 MPI at 50 kW; and GLX with 1.6 MPI at 55 kW or 1.9 D at 47 kW. These are entries in that market’s dated specification, not a general list of trim-to-engine availability in every country.[8]

A 1995 Š 791 1.6 MPI technical specification records a five-speed manual gearbox and front-wheel drive.[7]

Production revision

In February 1998 the hatchback received a major exterior update, including revised headlamps and grille. The facelift aligned the front styling with the larger Octavia and increased the hatchback’s overall length by 28 mm. This was a revision of type 791, not a separately named body style. Felicia production ended in June 2001.[1]

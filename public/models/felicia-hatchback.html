Škoda Felicia hatchback

The original Felicia was a five-door hatchback, factory type Š 791.[2] A 2014 Škoda Polska retrospective dates the first serial Felicia’s departure from Mladá Boleslav to 5 September 1994. Škoda’s 2019 account describes the 5 September run at Vrchlabí as pre-series production of 50 cars and dates regular production at Mladá Boleslav to 17 October. The sources classify the September milestone differently; Škoda presented the hatchback publicly in Prague on 26 October. The hatchback was the passenger body from which the Combi and commercial derivatives expanded the range.[1][6]

Identification

Š 791 is the factory model code for the five-door hatchback. In later German type-approval records it appears as Felicia type 791, with approval family G952 and EC approval e11*93/81*0011. The Combi is separately classified as 795. Pickup and Fun share the broad type number 797, but their approval and body designations distinguish the two derivatives.[3]

The body is a self-supporting five-door passenger shell with a rear liftgate. Škoda’s rescue sheet identifies the Felicia series as TMB-6U; it does not identify this hatchback as 6U1. TMB-6U is the rescue-document series identifier, separate from factory body code Š 791.[4]

Factory powertrain range

The initial 1.3-litre petrol engine used Bosch single-point injection and was rated at 40 or 50 kW. Škoda records 57 and 58 PS carburettor calibrations for some export markets. The 1.3 MPI versions arrived in 1996, at the same two rated outputs. Felicia engine codes identify fuel-supply and compression versions: 135B and 136B identify single-point-injection versions, while 135M and 136M identify multipoint-injection versions. A code or output is not a body identifier.[2][5][9]

The AEE 1.6 MPI petrol engine is rated at 55 kW. Škoda’s Polish importer dates its Felicia introduction to 1995 and records its first appearance with the Combi; a 2019 manufacturer retrospective lists the engine among additions in 1995. The naturally aspirated AEF 1.9 D is rated at 47 kW; Škoda Polska dates it to 1996, whereas the 2019 retrospective groups it with 1995 additions. These family-wide dates do not establish that every market offered each powertrain in the hatchback from the same month.[1][2][6]

A Dutch Škoda importer specification dated 1999 lists seven hatchback combinations: LX with 1.3 MPI at 40 kW, 1.6 MPI at 55 kW or 1.9 D at 47 kW; LXi with 1.3 MPI at 50 kW; GLXi with 1.3 MPI at 50 kW; and GLX with 1.6 MPI at 55 kW or 1.9 D at 47 kW. These are entries in that market’s dated specification, not a general list of trim-to-engine availability in every country.[8]

A 1995 Š 791 1.6 MPI technical specification records a five-speed manual gearbox and front-wheel drive.[7]

Production revision

Škoda’s 2014 Felicia retrospective places a first package of significant changes in 1996, including reinforcement of the body structure and multipoint injection for the 1.3-litre engine. It does not identify affected panels or give a body-specific chassis-number limit for the hatchback; code Š 791 alone therefore does not identify a reinforced-body parts range.[6]

A 2001 technical account describes a separate shell distinction for hatchbacks. Unadapted shells intended for cars without airbag provision were produced through December 1994. Production of shells adapted for airbags, air conditioning and Volkswagen engines began in January 1995 alongside them; unadapted production ended in July 1995, after which the account describes all shells as adapted. It also associates front and floor reinforcement with airbag-equipped cars and Volkswagen 1.6 MPI or 1.9 D engines. Škoda’s retrospective separately dates a body-structure change package to 1996. Neither account identifies changed panels or explains whether the 1996 package was an additional revision to the earlier shell adaptation. These are production-period descriptions, not VIN cutoffs or complete parts applications.[11][6]

Škoda dates a major Felicia facelift to February 1998. Its later technical history reports a 28 mm length increase for the hatchback. The body remains type 791 in later records, but that continuing type designation is not a production cutoff or proof that body parts remained interchangeable. Production ended in June 2001.[1][10]

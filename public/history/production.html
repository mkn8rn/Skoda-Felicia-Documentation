The production accounts distinguish passenger bodies, commercial derivatives and named editions. Published dates and totals retain the period, brand and source definitions used for each count.

Hatchback introduction

The original Felicia was a five-door hatchback, factory type Š 791.[1] A 2014 Škoda Polska retrospective dates the first serial Felicia’s departure from Mladá Boleslav to 5 September 1994. Škoda’s 2019 account describes the 5 September run at Vrchlabí as pre-series production of 50 cars and dates regular production at Mladá Boleslav to 17 October. The sources classify the September milestone differently; Škoda presented the hatchback publicly in Prague on 26 October. The hatchback was the passenger body from which the Combi and commercial derivatives expanded the range.[2][3]

Škoda presented the Combi in June 1995; production continued through 2001.[4]

Production totals

Škoda’s 2019 production account gives 915,843 hatchbacks, 351,905 Combi estates and 124,565 Pickups. It combines Fun and Vanplus as 9,176 vehicles; the 2020 Fun history divides that total into 4,016 Fun and 5,160 Vanplus vehicles. These figures sum to 1,401,489 Škoda-branded Felicias. Nearly 19,000 Volkswagen Caddy pickups, built between 1996 and 2000, are additional to that total.[2][5]

Commercial production accounts

Škoda’s 2019 history dates Pickup production at Kvasiny from August 1995; its 2014 retrospective places commercial-body production at Vrchlabí without dating that factory attribution. Neither source establishes whether production moved or overlapped between plants, or which builds are associated with each location.[2][3]

Škoda’s 2019 history dates Vanplus production at Kvasiny from August 1995; its 2014 retrospective places commercial-body production at Vrchlabí without dating that factory attribution. Neither source establishes whether production moved or overlapped between plants, or which builds are associated with each location. They do not date the two seating layouts separately.[2]

Fun production accounts

Series production ran from October 1995 to August 2000 at Vrchlabí.[6]

Škoda Polska’s 2014 retrospective reports 3,992 Fun vehicles; a 2020 Škoda Czech press brief gives 4,016, while the 2019 Škoda history rounds the series to approximately 4,000. These publications therefore preserve different reported production totals.[3][7]

Edition production and launch

The Felicia Laurin & Klement (L&K) was the range’s luxury equipment edition, introduced in 1995 for both the five-door hatchback and Combi. It marked the first modern Škoda production model to carry the historic L&K name. The edition celebrated the 100th anniversary of the company’s foundation and was positioned above the regular Felicia equipment versions.[2][8]

Škoda reported 3,806 Felicia L&K cars: 1,477 hatchbacks and 2,329 Combi estates. The L&K treatment therefore spans two independently identifiable Felicia body types and does not replace their body-specific identification.[8]

Škoda announced Felicia Magic for the Czech domestic market in the second half of September 1998. The press announcement set a planned series of 3,000 vehicles and identified granite metallic as the series paint. It named both Felicia and Felicia Combi; the contemporary launch notice documents both bodies and assigns Combi prices to the 40 and 50 kW outputs.[9]

Škoda Auto Polska’s account published on 28 September 2003 reports nearly 3,500 Felicia Samba sales. This is the importer’s rounded sales figure as of that account, without a body/engine split or a separate factory production total.[12]

Facelift and production end

Škoda dates the passenger-car facelift to February 1998.[10] Production continued into 2001; the hatchback’s production ended in June.[2]

Kit Car programme

Škoda developed the Kit Car in 1995 for the international Formula 2 and kit-car classes. Unlike a road Felicia, the competition car was homologated and prepared for rally regulations; its specifications cannot be transferred to ordinary type 791 hatchbacks or other road models.[11]

Factory histories describe 1.3-litre and 1.5-litre competition engines in the first Kit Car specification. Later cars used a 1.6-litre engine developed by Škoda Motorsport, with output reported up to 174 hp. These competition configurations were separate from road Felicias, which used 1.3 MPI, Volkswagen-group AEE 1.6 MPI and AEF 1.9 D power units.[11][4]

The Kit Car competed in the World Rally Championship from 1995 through 1997. Škoda records a class third place in its first season, a 1996 RAC Rally third place overall and first in the Kit Car class up to 2.0 litres for Stig Blomqvist and Benny Melander, and a 1997 second place in its class in the championship. The Kit Car programme followed the Felicia road range but is counted separately from its production totals.[2]

French Morzine announcement

A January 1999 period notice announced Morzine in passenger and estate forms. It gave a range starting price of FRF 55,190 (€8,413), without a body or engine price allocation. The accessories described were a portable cooler/warmer and child booster seat; the notice did not state a production quantity.[13]

Right-hand-drive production certificate

The production certificate attached to A-5528 supply variant 06/02 VF identifies Felicia with right steering and certifies 2,703 completed cars in April 1995. This is the certificate’s one-month production figure, not the lifetime total for right-hand-drive Felicias. It does not divide the cars by national grade, engine code or original part selection. The variant’s separate homologation validity begins on 1 July 1995.[14]

Named historical-vehicle accounts

The timeline labels its Kit Car 1500 illustration 1996 and gives a 1994–1996 range. That retrospective range does not identify an individual build or homologation extension. Its Golden Prague illustration is labelled 1998 and records one vehicle, without identifying a commercial production series.[17]

The timeline associates both the ordinary Combi and its Combi LX police entry with 351,895 cars. The 2022 service history instead gives the Combi family total as 351,905. These are family figures, not a count of one police configuration.[17][18]

The 2022 history says the Czech police used more than 5,500 Felicia Combi estates; it does not divide that fleet by engine, grade or conversion. The same retrospective identifies Felicia Combi ambulance versions in the 1995–2001 Combi family context, without dating the medical conversions or their individual production.[18]

Body-conversion accounts

MTX’s retrospective places the Felicia cabriolet after its Rapid conversions and Favorit two-seat Roadster. It describes Felicia cabriolet manufacture continuing close to the end of the twentieth century, without giving Felicia-specific start/end dates or a production total. The 1990 start of MTX’s convertible programme is not a Felicia production date.[19]

Laureta’s corporate history describes a J. Laureta utility-vehicle manufacturing project based on Škoda Pickup around the turn of the millennium. The ministry separately identifies HP-0026 as an extended-body Felicia Pickup conversion. These accounts do not establish that all manufactured J. Laureta vehicles and all HP-0026 conversions have the same donor specification or body assembly.[20][21]

Swedish sales and registration accounts

Škoda’s Swedish importer reported in November 2004 that 400 Felicias were first registered in Sweden in 1995, followed by the Combi and Pickup introductions in 1996. Its 4,000 registrations for 1996 and 100,000-car import milestone in 2004 concern the Škoda brand, not Felicia-only production or sales.[22]

The July 2007 Pickup retrospective reports Swedish Felicia Pickup sales during 1996–2000 and more than 17,000 Pickups sold in Sweden. This is an importer sales statement, not a global factory total or an engine/grade/build breakdown. The subsequent Praktik safety specifications do not describe Felicia equipment.[23]

April 2009 manufacturer aggregate

An April 2009 Škoda manufacturer account reports 1,420,441 Felicia vehicles for 1994–2001 without a body or badge breakdown. Its aggregate is separate from the explicitly summed Škoda-branded 1,401,489 total in the 2019 account; the older figure alone does not identify which derivatives or rebadged vehicles it includes.[24]

Estonian 1995 importer-sales account

Äripäev’s November 2005 importer history reports that Eesti Helkama-Auto sold 193 Felicias and 49 Felicia Combis in 1995. These are the named importer’s reported sales, not global factory totals or a grade/engine/equipment breakdown. Its separate June 1996 figure of 69 new cars concerns the importer’s overall monthly sales, without a Felicia-only split.[26]

Slovenian May 1995 test offer

The Gorenjski glas test records 1,442,000 Slovenian tolars before registration for its 50 kW Felicia, with Avtoimpex in Ljubljana named. The report is dated 16 May 1995 and labels the test GLXi in the headline but GLX in the body. This price is a dated reported offer, not a production count or grade-wide parts selector.[15]

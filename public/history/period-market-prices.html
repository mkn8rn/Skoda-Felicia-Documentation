Felicia period market prices

Period reports identify dated launch prices, paint offers and Greek-market grade prices. A quoted price or option charge does not establish a production limit or a complete fitted equipment set.

Spanish November 1994 announcement

Motor16 reports an announced range of 1,050,000–1,370,000 Spanish pesetas, from the least expensive to the most equipped Felicia. It names LX/GLX grades and two engine columns without mapping each price endpoint to an exact grade/engine configuration. The announcement is not proof of a delivery date.[2]

Colombia, September 1996

Semana’s 16 September 1996 report gives COP 14,500,000 for the GLX 1.6 solid-paint offer and COP 14,800,000 for metallic paint. The difference is COP 300,000. Colour codes, tax basis, dealer adjustments and manufacturing limits are Unconfirmed.[1]

Greece, June 1996

The Greek price rows give GRD 3,590,000 for Felicia GLXi 1.6i, GRD 3,790,000 for Combi LXi 1.6i and GRD 3,990,000 for Combi GLXi 1.6i. The tested GLXi cars include ABS, so the basic grade price does not define their final equipment cost. The separately described 1.3 Pickup is quoted at GRD 2,250,000 without registration plates; the report gives additional registration charges of GRD 120,000 for commercial or GRD 80,000 for agricultural licensing. These charges do not identify different engines or fitted parts.[3]

Austrian GLX 1.6 test price

The Austrian GLX 1.6 test reports a price of 156,990 schillings. Its archive labels model year 1998 and publishes the web article on 2 January 2001. The archive also displays a euro amount without establishing the original pricing date or its relation to the schilling price. Included taxes and dated order/price-list validity remain Unconfirmed.[13]

Norway, 1998 period prices

The 8 June importer report gives NOK 129,900 for the Felicia LXi estate after a stated 7.14% reduction. It does not identify the estate’s engine. The separately mentioned price gap between an unnamed entry hatchback and estate does not establish a hatchback grade.[14]

The October BIL LXi 1.3 test lists NOK 126,500. This is a different dated hatchback test offer, not an engine or equipment mapping for the June estate price.[15]

Romania, December 1996 importer prices

The 24 December advertisement lists DM 12,980 for LX 1.3 carburettor 40 kW, DM 14,800 for LXi 1.3 injection 50 kW, and DM 18,200 for each GLX 1.6 injection/catalyst 55 kW and Combi LX 1.6 injection/catalyst 55 kW. It states that customs duties and VAT are included. These quotations do not establish engine codes, complete equipment or manufacturing applicability.[16]

Romania, November 1998 dealer prices

The 3 November advertisement gives DM 14,017 for Felicia GLXi and DM 14,423 for Felicia Combi LXi, without identifying either engine. The promotion is directed at private legal entities increasing their capital under the stated historical scheme. That buyer qualification does not identify different fitted parts or a factory edition.[17]

Romania, February 1997

Compexit’s advertisement gives DM 14,278 for the Felicia 1.3 offer and DM 18,575 for the Felicia 1.6 injection-and-catalyst offer, including taxes and customs charges. These are dated offers without a grade, complete body/engine identity or original equipment map.[18]

Moscow, November 1997

The period catalogue quotes new-car showroom prices in US dollars for customs-cleared cars with minimum equipment. A second US-dollar column compares the named offers with Germany; it does not establish German-specific engine, equipment or manufacturing identity.[19]

Greece, summer 1998

The GLX 1.3 comparison quotes GRD 3,750,000 and describes fitted air conditioning/electric windows during promotional offers. It does not establish the duration, factory option codes or tax/charge basis of the offer.[4]

Greece, November 1999

The report describes a basic Felicia price below GRD 2,500,000 and the tested configuration with air conditioning, ABS and a driver airbag as costing no more than GRD 3,100,000. These are bounds rather than exact quoted transaction prices and do not identify the basic car’s engine/grade or the tested car’s option codes.[5]

Slovenia, March 1995

The GLXi test quotes SIT 1,486,500 from Avtoimpex d.o.o., Ljubljana. A quoted test price does not determine factory component selection.[6]

At the June Combi launch, the publisher explicitly reports that the Slovenian price had not yet been set; no Combi price is derived from the March hatchback quotation.[7]

Spain, February 1995 test offer

The GLX comparison quotes ESP 1,210,000. The same report describes a Felicia delivery-delay promotion offering either an ESP 100,000 reduction or two front airbags. It does not tie that general promotion to a specific GLX engine/product configuration or establish fitted airbags on the photographed test car.[8]

Spain, May 1995 price list

The 30 May list gives ESP 1,065,000 for LXi, ESP 1,110,000 for LXi Plus and ESP 1,210,000 for GLXi. For all three it separately prices air conditioning at ESP 150,000, metallic paint at ESP 27,500, ABS at ESP 100,000, alloy wheels at ESP 52,000 and a driver airbag at ESP 100,000. The guide defines these public prices for mainland Spain and the Balearic Islands as including VAT, registration tax and transport, with registration procedures and insurance additional. Prices do not establish fitted options, combined packages or manufacturer production/option codes.[9]

Spain, June 1995 Combi forecast

The June launch account forecasts September arrival in Spain. It estimates the cheapest Combi slightly below ESP 1,300,000 and the 1.6 around ESP 1,500,000, while explicitly saying it is too early to give prices. These estimates are not a dated Spanish tariff, actual transaction or proved engine/grade offer.[10]

Portugal, 1997–1998 advertisements

The August 1997 advertisement lists PVP 2150 contos for Felicia 1.3 GLXi, 3480 contos for Felicia 1.9 GLXD, 2350 contos for Break 1.3 GLXi and 3680 contos for Break 1.9 GLXD. It does not specify a tax/registration/transport basis or an adapted-control package. Those price boxes recur in the separately dated March and May 1998 advertisements; unchanged advertised prices are not continuous sale or manufacturing proof.[11]

Hungary, May 1996 dealer advertisement

Autó Szremkó’s 9 May advertisement places a starting figure of HUF 1599000 alongside the Combi illustration and HUF 1349000 alongside the hatchback illustration. It does not name a grade, output, engine or fitted equipment for either amount. The illustrated cars are not complete configuration selectors.[12]
